📈 Property Tax Analysis for Minnesota

Minnesota’s property tax landscape is anchored by an average effective tax rate of 0.96%, slightly above the national average of 0.90%. This modest premium places the state in the upper tier of tax burdens relative to peers, though it remains moderate compared with high‑tax jurisdictions such as New Jersey or Illinois. The average median property tax of $2,287, derived from a median home value of $238,335, translates to roughly 0.96% of assessed value, confirming the rate’s consistency across the 87 counties. Local variance, however, is pronounced. While the statewide figure provides a useful benchmark, individual counties may levy rates ranging from under 0.70% to over 1.30%, and municipal assessments can differ further based on property classification, school district levies, and special district funding. County-level millage rates, which are expressed in mills per thousand dollars of assessed value, can differ markedly, with some jurisdictions imposing additional levies for fire protection or road maintenance that further shape the final tax bill. Consequently, two homes of comparable market value may bear substantially different tax liabilities depending on their jurisdiction. Most Minnesota counties employ standard exemption mechanisms, including homestead credits, senior citizen abatements, and veteran exclusions, which directly reduce taxable valuation. Additionally, property owners retain the statutory right to appeal assessed values, a process that often mitigates perceived overvaluation. For prospective buyers, the effective rate’s proximity to the national norm suggests that purchasing power is not dramatically constrained by tax considerations alone. Current homeowners should monitor annual levy adjustments, reassess exemption eligibility, and consider appeal options if assessments appear inflated. Given the median household income of $77,500, the median tax burden represents approximately 2.95% of income, a figure that, while manageable, warrants periodic review to align with any changes in income or property valuation.

Based on aggregated U.S. Census ACS 5-Year Estimates across all 87 counties.
