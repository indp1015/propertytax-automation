📝 Property Tax Notes

The effective property‑tax rate in Grant County stands at 0.72 %, which is 0.20 percentage points below the national average of 0.92 % and 0.72 percentage points above the reported Indiana state average (listed as 0.00 %). Relative to the broader United States, the county can be classified as a low‑tax jurisdiction; the rate is roughly 22 % lower than the national benchmark. The median annual levy of $884 on a home valued at $122,300 further underscores the modest fiscal demand compared with the national median tax of $1,630. When expressed as a share of median household income ($53,522), the property‑tax burden equals 1.65 % of earnings. This proportion is well under the 3 %–4 % range that many housing‑affordability studies cite as a threshold for sustainable tax loads. At this level, a typical household can meet the levy without jeopardizing other essential expenses, indicating that the current tax structure is financially manageable for most residents. Over a 30‑year horizon, the cumulative tax outlay on a median‑priced home totals $26,520 (30 × $884). This long‑term cost represents roughly half a percent of the home’s original value and remains modest relative to the total cost of ownership when mortgage principal, insurance, and maintenance are considered. Prospective buyers should therefore factor property tax as a relatively minor component of monthly housing costs, but they should also verify any local assessments that could differ from the county median. Current owners may benefit from statewide programs such as the Homestead Tax Credit, senior exemptions, or agricultural assessments where applicable. Participation in these programs can further reduce the effective rate, enhancing the county’s appeal to retirees and low‑income households. **Insight:** Although Grant County’s effective rate is low, the reported “Indiana State Average Rate: 0.00 %” likely reflects a data‑entry placeholder rather than a true zero rate; the county’s 0.72 % is actually higher than the functional state average, which typically hovers around 0.70 %. This nuance clarifies that Grant County is only marginally above the true state norm despite appearing dramatically higher in the presented comparison.
