📝 Property Tax Notes

Cumberland County presents a notably low property tax environment relative to both national and state benchmarks. The effective rate of 0.54% falls 38 basis points below the national average of 0.92% and exceeds the reported state average by 54 basis points, positioning the county as a low-tax jurisdiction. The median annual tax of $760 represents less than half the national median of $1,630, driven by a modest median home value of $141,100. However, affordability analysis reveals a more nuanced picture: property taxes consume 1.90% of the $40,000 median household income, a meaningful burden in a lower-income community where housing costs compete with other essential expenditures. Over a 30-year ownership horizon, the cumulative tax obligation on a median-valued property totals approximately $22,800, assuming static assessments and rates. For prospective buyers, this long-term cost should be modeled alongside mortgage principal and interest, as the relatively low annual outlay may understate the total cost of ownership when insurance, maintenance, and potential reassessment are considered. Current owners should verify assessment accuracy annually; Kentucky's homestead exemption for owners aged 65 or older or those classified as totally disabled can reduce assessed value by $46,350 (2024 amount), yielding meaningful savings. Agricultural or horticultural land may qualify for preferential valuation under KRS 132.450. A concrete insight: despite the low nominal rate, the tax-to-income ratio of 1.90% exceeds the national average of roughly 1.1%, indicating that Cumberland County's property tax system is regressive relative to local earning capacity. The low absolute tax bill masks a disproportionate burden on households least positioned to absorb it.
