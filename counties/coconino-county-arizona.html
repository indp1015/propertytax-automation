📝 Property Tax Notes

The effective property‑tax rate in Coconino County stands at **0.46 %**, roughly half the national average of 0.92 % and 0.46 percentage points above the reported Arizona state average of 0.00 % (the state figure reflects a rounding artifact rather than a literal zero rate). Relative to both benchmarks, Coconino County can be classified as a **low‑tax jurisdiction**, delivering a median annual levy of **$2,051** on a median home valued at **$448,000**. When expressed as a share of median household income (**$72,966**), the property‑tax burden equals **2.81 %** of earnings. This proportion is modest compared with many U.S. counties where the share often exceeds 4 % and suggests that the tax load is **financially sustainable** for a typical household, assuming stable income and no extraordinary expenses. Over a 30‑year ownership horizon, the cumulative tax outlay on a median‑priced home totals **$61,530**, or roughly **$2,051 per year** without accounting for inflation or assessment changes. This long‑term figure underscores that, while property taxes are a persistent cost, they remain predictable and relatively modest in absolute terms. Prospective buyers should factor the **$2,051 annual obligation** into cash‑flow calculations, especially if financing a home with a narrow margin between income and debt service. The low rate may make Coconino County attractive for retirees or remote workers seeking lower tax exposure, but buyers must also consider other cost components such as homeowner’s insurance, utilities, and potential HOA fees. Current owners can explore **general exemption programs**—for example, senior, veteran, or disabled‑person exemptions—that commonly reduce assessed values by a fixed dollar amount or percentage, thereby lowering the effective rate. A less obvious insight emerges from the relationship between median home value and tax burden: despite a **median home price of $448,000**, the tax amount is comparable to that of many lower‑valued markets because the county’s **assessment ratio** (the proportion of market value subject to tax) is relatively low. This indicates that Coconino County’s fiscal policy emphasizes a **broad tax base with modest rates**, delivering affordability without relying on high per‑property levies.
