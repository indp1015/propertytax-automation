📝 Property Tax Notes

Dorchester County's effective property tax rate of 0.94% positions it marginally above the national average of 0.92% and significantly above the reported state average of 0.00%, though the latter figure likely reflects data reporting anomalies rather than actual policy. The median annual tax of $2,456 exceeds the national median of $1,630 by 50.7%, driven by the county's moderate median home value of $262,400. This classifies Dorchester as a moderately high-tax jurisdiction relative to national benchmarks, though not extreme within the Mid-Atlantic region. Affordability analysis reveals a meaningful burden: property taxes consume 3.97% of median household income ($61,839), approaching the 4% threshold where housing cost pressure becomes acute for median earners. Over a 30-year ownership horizon, cumulative property tax liability on a median-priced home totals $73,680—equivalent to 119% of one year's median household income. This long-term cost represents a substantial wealth transfer that prospective buyers must factor into total cost-of-ownership calculations alongside mortgage interest, insurance, and maintenance. Prospective homeowners should note that Dorchester's tax burden, while manageable at median income levels, disproportionately affects fixed-income households and those near the median threshold. Current owners should review eligibility for Maryland's Homeowners' Property Tax Credit, Homestead Tax Credit limiting assessment increases, and potential senior or veteran exemptions. A critical insight: the county's relatively low median home value combined with a near-national-average rate creates a "hidden" affordability trap—buyers purchasing above the median price point face rapidly escalating tax liabilities without corresponding income growth, as the tax burden scales linearly with assessment while household incomes in the county remain constrained.
