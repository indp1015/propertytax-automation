📝 Property Tax Notes

The effective property‑tax rate in Suffolk County (0.66 %) sits 0.26 percentage points below the national average of 0.92 % and 0.66 percentage points above the reported Massachusetts state average of 0.00 % (the state figure reflects a reporting artifact rather than a true zero rate). Relative to most U.S. jurisdictions, Suffolk County is a low‑tax area; its median annual levy of $4,649 is nearly three times the national median tax of $1,630, but this difference is driven by the county’s high median home value ($705,800) rather than a higher rate. The tax burden therefore reflects property wealth rather than an aggressive levy structure. Property‑tax expense consumes 4.86 % of median household income ($95,631), a proportion that aligns with the national range of 3‑6 % for residential property taxes. At this share, the outlay is generally sustainable for households earning the median income, leaving ample margin for other housing costs such as mortgage principal, insurance, and utilities. The 5‑year, 10‑year, and 30‑year cumulative figures ($23,245; $46,490; $139,470) illustrate that, over a typical 30‑year mortgage horizon, property tax will represent a sizable but predictable component of total housing expense. Prospective buyers should factor the $4,649 annual levy into cash‑flow calculations, especially when evaluating affordability on a median‑priced home. The relatively modest 0.66 % rate means that even modest increases in assessed value will not cause disproportionate spikes in tax bills, but buyers should verify the municipality’s assessment practices and any pending levy changes. Current owners can explore general Massachusetts relief programs such as the Home Energy Assistance Program (HEAP) or the Senior Circuit Breaker Tax Credit, which may offset a portion of the levy for eligible households; participation in local assessment appeals or exemptions for historic properties can also reduce the effective rate. **Concrete insight:** Although Suffolk County’s median tax is high in dollar terms, the low effective rate indicates that the jurisdiction’s fiscal burden is driven by property values rather than tax intensity, suggesting that any policy aimed at reducing the dollar amount would need to address assessment levels rather than rate adjustments.
