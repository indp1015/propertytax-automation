📝 Property Tax Notes

The effective property‑tax rate in Marion County stands at 1.22 %, which is 0.30 percentage points above the national average of 0.92 % and 1.22 percentage points higher than the Iowa state average (reported as 0.00 %). This places the county firmly in the higher‑tax tier relative to both benchmarks; the rate exceeds the national norm by roughly 33 % and is the highest among the three reference points. Consequently, Marion County should be classified as a high‑tax jurisdiction for residential property owners. When the median household income of $78,680 is paired with the median annual property tax of $2,848, property taxes consume 3.62 % of household earnings. This share is modest compared with other expense categories—such as housing costs, which typically range from 25‑30 % of income—but it is notably above the national average property‑tax burden of about 2 % of income. At the current level, the tax share remains financially sustainable for median‑income families, provided that other cost components (mortgage, utilities, insurance) do not push total housing expenditures beyond conventional affordability thresholds. Over a 30‑year horizon, a homeowner of a median‑priced residence ($233,000) would pay $85,440 in property taxes, assuming the $2,848 annual amount remains constant. This cumulative figure represents roughly 12 % of the home’s original value and illustrates the long‑term fiscal weight of the county’s tax structure. Prospective buyers should therefore incorporate an estimated $2,800‑plus annual tax line item into cash‑flow analyses and compare it against alternative markets where rates are nearer the national average. Current owners can explore statewide relief programs such as the Iowa Homestead Credit, senior exemptions, or agricultural‑use assessments, which may reduce the effective rate. While eligibility varies, participation in these programs can lower the nominal 1.22 % rate by several tenths of a percentage point, directly improving affordability. **Insight:** Although the nominal tax rate is high, the absolute median tax bill ($2,848) remains below the national median tax of $1,630 multiplied by the higher local home value, indicating that the county’s higher rate is largely a function of property‑value levels rather than an aggressive levy on low‑valued parcels.
