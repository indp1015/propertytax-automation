📝 Property Tax Notes

The effective property‑tax rate in Ouray County stands at 0.28 %, which is 0.64 percentage points lower than the national average of 0.92 % and 0.28 percentage points above the reported Colorado state average of 0.00 % (the state figure reflects Colorado’s statewide cap of 0.0 % on the portion of the tax that is not earmarked for specific levies). Relative to both benchmarks, Ouray County can be classified as a low‑tax jurisdiction; the rate is roughly one‑third of the national norm and the median annual tax of $2,050 is $420 below the national median of $1,630. When expressed as a share of median household income ($91,020), the annual property tax consumes 2.25 % of earnings. This proportion is modest compared with many U.S. metros where property‑tax burdens often exceed 3 % of income, suggesting that the tax load is sustainable for median‑income households. The 5‑year, 10‑year, and 30‑year cumulative figures—$10,250, $20,500, and $61,500 respectively—confirm that even over three decades the total tax outlay remains well under 10 % of the median home’s value ($739,800). Prospective buyers should factor the $2,050 annual levy into cash‑flow calculations but can expect a relatively predictable expense given the low rate and the county’s historically stable assessment practices. Buyers of higher‑priced homes will see the dollar amount rise proportionally, yet the rate itself will not change without a voter‑approved mill levy. Current owners may benefit from general Colorado exemptions such as the homestead exemption, senior‑citizen relief, or agricultural use classifications, which can further reduce the taxable base. A less obvious insight emerges from the disparity between the low effective rate and the relatively high median home value: the county’s fiscal capacity relies heavily on a modest tax rate applied to a high‑value property pool, allowing residents to enjoy low‑tax pressure while the jurisdiction maintains sufficient revenue for services.
