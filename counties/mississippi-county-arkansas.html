📝 Property Tax Notes

The effective property‑tax rate in Mississippi County stands at 0.52 %, which is 0.40 percentage points below the national average of 0.92 % and 0.52 percentage points above the reported state figure of 0.00 % (the Arkansas state average appears to be rounded to zero in the source data). Relative to both benchmarks, Mississippi County can be classified as a low‑tax jurisdiction; the rate is roughly one‑half of the national norm and markedly lower than many neighboring counties that often exceed 0.70 %. With a median household income of $52,548, the median annual property tax of $635 represents 1.21 % of income. This share is modest when compared with the national median tax‑to‑income ratio, which typically ranges from 2 % to 3 % in higher‑tax states. At 1.21 %, the burden is well within sustainable limits for most households, leaving ample margin for other essential expenses such as utilities, transportation, and education. Over a 30‑year horizon, a homeowner of a median‑priced residence ($122,500) would pay a cumulative $19,050 in property taxes, assuming the current rate and valuation remain constant. This long‑term cost translates to roughly 15.5 % of the original purchase price, a relatively small component of total home‑ownership expenses when compared with typical mortgage interest, insurance, and maintenance outlays. Prospective buyers should note that the low tax rate contributes to overall housing affordability, but they must also consider that the county’s modest tax base may limit funding for public services such as road maintenance and schools. Buyers should evaluate the quality and availability of these services alongside the tax advantage. Current owners can explore general exemption programs that many Arkansas jurisdictions offer, including homestead, senior, and veteran exemptions, which can further reduce the effective rate. Participation in local assessment appeals, where property values are contested, may also yield savings. A less obvious insight is that the low effective rate, combined with a median home value of $122,500, yields a property‑tax burden that is disproportionately lower than the national average even after adjusting for home‑price differences; this suggests that Mississippi County’s tax structure is particularly favorable for owners of modest‑priced homes, enhancing long‑term affordability beyond what the headline rate alone conveys.
