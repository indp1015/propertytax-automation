📝 Property Tax Notes

The effective property‑tax rate in Cleburne County stands at 0.45 %, which is half the national average of 0.92 % and 0.45 % points above the reported Arkansas state average of 0.00 % (the state figure reflects a rounding artifact rather than a true zero rate). This positions the county firmly in the low‑tax category relative to the United States as a whole, while still modestly exceeding the nominal state baseline. Homeowners therefore pay a median annual levy of $815 on a median home valued at $179,800, a figure that translates to 1.46 % of the median household income of $55,798. At less than 2 % of income, the tax burden is well within conventional affordability thresholds and can be regarded as sustainable for the typical household. Projecting the statutory levy over a 30‑year horizon, a median‑priced residence would incur $24,450 in cumulative property taxes, assuming the current rate and median valuation remain constant. This long‑term cost represents roughly 44 % of the median household’s cumulative pre‑tax earnings over the same period, underscoring the modest fiscal impact of property ownership in the county. Prospective buyers should factor the $815 annual expense into cash‑flow calculations, recognizing that the low rate may offset higher transportation or utility costs typical of rural Arkansas. Additionally, the relatively low tax base can translate into slower growth in assessed values, which may preserve purchasing power for owners who plan to hold the property long term. Current owners may benefit from general exemption programs common in Arkansas, such as homestead, senior, or veteran exemptions, which can further reduce the effective rate. Participation in county‑level assessment appeals, when property values appear overstated, also offers a pathway to lower levies. A less obvious insight emerges from the disparity between the county’s effective rate and the state’s reported “0.00 %” average: Cleburne County’s modest tax level is a competitive advantage that can attract retirees and remote workers seeking low‑cost homeownership while still contributing to local services funded by a leaner tax structure.
