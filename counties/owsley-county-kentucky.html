📝 Property Tax Notes

Owsley County presents a distinctive property tax profile characterized by a low nominal burden but elevated relative affordability pressure. The county's effective tax rate of 0.71% sits 21 basis points below the national average of 0.92%, positioning it as a low-rate jurisdiction in absolute terms. However, the state comparison requires context: Kentucky's reported 0.00% average appears to reflect a data limitation rather than a true zero rate, making the county's +0.71% differential difficult to interpret meaningfully against state benchmarks. The median annual tax of $570 represents roughly 35% of the national median ($1,630), driven primarily by the county's modest median home value of $80,100. Affordability analysis reveals significant strain despite the low dollar amount. Property taxes consume 2.57% of median household income ($22,188), a ratio that exceeds common affordability thresholds and suggests sustainability concerns for typical residents. Over a 30-year ownership horizon, cumulative property taxes on a median-priced home total $17,100—equivalent to 77% of a single year's median household income. This long-term burden underscores how low home values and low incomes interact to create disproportionate tax pressure. Prospective buyers should recognize that while the $570 annual obligation appears manageable, it represents a meaningful claim on local purchasing power. Current owners may benefit from exploring Kentucky's homestead exemption for seniors and disabled homeowners, as well as agricultural or conservation valuation programs where applicable. A critical insight not evident from aggregate figures: the county's extremely low median home value means that even minor assessment increases translate into large percentage tax hikes for owners, creating volatility that fixed-income households are poorly positioned to absorb.
