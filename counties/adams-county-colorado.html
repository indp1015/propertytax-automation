📝 Property Tax Notes

The effective property‑tax rate in Adams County is 0.61 %, which sits 0.31 percentage points below the national average of 0.92 % and 0.61 percentage points above the reported Colorado state average of 0.00 %. Relative to the broader United States, the county can be classified as a low‑tax jurisdiction; the rate is roughly two‑thirds of the national norm. The median annual levy of $2,962 also exceeds the national median tax of $1,630, reflecting the county’s higher home values rather than a higher rate per dollar of assessed value. When expressed as a share of median household income ($94,571), the property‑tax burden equals 3.13 % of earnings. This proportion is modest compared with many high‑tax metros where property taxes can consume 5 % or more of income, suggesting that the current level is financially sustainable for median‑income families. The 5‑year, 10‑year and 30‑year cumulative figures—$14,810, $29,620 and $88,860 respectively—illustrate that, over a typical home‑ownership horizon, property taxes represent a predictable, though sizable, expense. For prospective buyers, the key considerations are the absolute tax dollar amount and its interaction with mortgage costs. A median‑priced home ($484,200) will generate an annual tax bill near $3,000, which should be factored into the total monthly housing outlay. Buyers should also evaluate the county’s assessment practices and any available homestead or senior exemptions that could lower the taxable base. Current owners can explore general relief programs such as the Colorado Property Tax/Rent Rebate, agricultural or wildfire‑mitigation exemptions, and the possibility of appealing assessed values if they believe the appraisal exceeds market value. **Insight:** Although Adams County’s tax rate is lower than the national average, the higher median home price pushes the median tax bill above the national median. Consequently, the county’s overall tax burden is driven more by property values than by the rate itself, highlighting the importance of assessing both components when evaluating affordability.
