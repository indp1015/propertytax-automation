📝 Property Tax Notes

The effective property‑tax rate in Cass County (0.56 %) sits 0.36 percentage points below the national average of 0.92 % and 0.56 percentage points above the reported Minnesota state average of 0.00 % (the state figure reflects a reporting artifact rather than a true zero rate). Relative to other U.S. jurisdictions, Cass County is a low‑tax area; the median annual levy of $1,615 is marginally under the national median of $1,630 and well below the amounts paid in many high‑rate counties where rates exceed 1 %. At a median household income of $68,874, the $1,615 tax represents 2.34 % of earnings. This share is modest when compared with the national average property‑tax burden, which typically ranges from 3 % to 4 % of income in higher‑tax states. A 2.34 % allocation suggests that the tax load is sustainable for the median household, leaving ample margin for other housing costs such as mortgage principal, insurance, and maintenance. Over a 30‑year ownership horizon, a median‑priced home ($288,300) would incur $48,450 in cumulative property taxes, assuming the current rate and median levy remain constant. This figure translates to roughly 16.8 % of the home’s original purchase price, a relatively low proportion of total ownership cost relative to many parts of the country where 30‑year tax payments can exceed 25 % of purchase price. Prospective buyers should note that the low effective rate reduces the upfront cash requirement for annual taxes, but they must still budget for potential reassessments that could raise the assessed value and, consequently, the tax bill. Current owners may benefit from statewide programs such as the Homestead Tax Credit, the Senior Citizen Property Tax Deferral, and agricultural or forest land exemptions where applicable; participation can further lower the effective rate. A less obvious insight is that the modest tax burden, combined with a median home value well below the national median, creates a favorable tax‑to‑value ratio (approximately $5.60 of tax per $1,000 of assessed value). This ratio positions Cass County as an attractive market for investors seeking low‑tax, affordable‑price properties with limited exposure to steep tax escalations.
