📝 Property Tax Notes

The effective property‑tax rate in Graves County stands at 0.66 %, which is 0.26 percentage points below the national average of 0.92 % and 0.66 percentage points above the reported Kentucky state average of 0.00 % (the state figure reflects a rounding artifact rather than a true zero rate). Relative to other jurisdictions, Graves County can be classified as a low‑tax area; the median annual levy of $970 is roughly 40 % lower than the national median tax of $1,630, indicating a comparatively modest fiscal burden on homeowners. When expressed as a share of median household income, the property‑tax expense equals 1.91 % ($970 ÷ $50,772 × 100). This proportion is well under the often‑cited affordability threshold of 3 % for housing‑related taxes, suggesting that the current levy is sustainable for a typical household. The 5‑year cumulative tax of $4,850 and the 10‑year total of $9,700 reinforce the modest nature of the burden, especially when juxtaposed with the median home value of $147,800. Over a 30‑year ownership horizon, a median‑priced residence would incur $29,100 in property taxes (30 × $970). This amount represents roughly 19.7 % of the home’s original value, a relatively low proportion compared with many higher‑tax counties where the 30‑year tax share can exceed 30 % of the purchase price. Prospective buyers should therefore factor in the lower recurring cost when evaluating overall affordability, but also verify any local assessment practices that could alter future rates. Current owners may benefit from state‑wide programs such as the Kentucky Homestead Exemption, which can reduce taxable assessed value for primary residences, and from agricultural or senior‑citizen exemptions where applicable. While eligibility criteria vary, these mechanisms can further lower the effective rate. **Concrete insight:** The disparity between the county’s 0.66 % effective rate and the national 0.92 % rate translates into an annual savings of about $660 per $100,000 of assessed value, meaning that a homeowner purchasing a $200,000 home in Graves County would save roughly $1,320 each year relative to a typical high‑tax county. This hidden savings component can materially improve long‑term net‑worth accumulation.
