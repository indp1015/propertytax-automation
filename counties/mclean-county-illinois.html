Market Analysis

McLean County's effective property tax rate of 2.17% is 20.8% above the Illinois state average and 136.9% above the U.S. national average. At this rate, a homeowner with a $400,000 property would pay approximately $8,680 per year in property taxes.

📝 Property Tax Notes

McLean County's effective property tax rate of 2.17% substantially exceeds both the national average of 0.92% and the Illinois state average, positioning it as a high-tax jurisdiction. The median annual tax of $4,524 is nearly 2.8 times the national median of $1,630, despite a median home value of $208,400 that sits below the national median. This rate differential of 1.25 percentage points above the national benchmark translates to approximately $2,600 in additional annual tax burden on a median-valued property compared to a national-average jurisdiction. The affordability impact is significant: property taxes consume 5.66% of median household income ($79,905), a threshold that exceeds common affordability benchmarks of 2-3%. Over a 30-year ownership period, cumulative property taxes on a median-priced home reach $135,720—equivalent to 65% of the current median home value and 170% of median annual household income. This long-term cost projection assumes static valuations and rates; reassessments and levy increases would compound the burden further. Prospective buyers should factor the effective rate into total cost-of-ownership calculations, recognizing that the $4,524 annual obligation represents a fixed cost irrespective of mortgage status. Current owners should verify assessment accuracy annually, as Illinois' triennial reassessment cycle creates regular appeal windows. The General Homestead Exemption ($6,000 assessed value reduction) and Senior Homestead Exemption ($5,000 reduction) provide baseline relief, while the Senior Freeze Exemption caps assessed values for qualifying households earning under $65,000. A non-obvious insight: McLean County's tax burden is driven less by home values and more by the concentration of taxing bodies—over 15 overlapping districts (county, municipality, township, school, park, library, fire, etc.) each levy independently, creating a cumulative rate structure where no single entity controls the total burden.
