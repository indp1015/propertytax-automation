Morehouse Parish presents a distinctly low-tax environment relative to national benchmarks. The median annual tax obligation of $530 represents roughly one-third of the national median ($1,630). While the comparative data indicates a state average rate of 0.00%, this figure likely reflects structural reporting nuances rather than an absence of property taxation statewide; regardless, Morehouse Parish's rate exceeds that baseline by 45 basis points. Affordability metrics reveal a moderate burden relative to local income levels. Property taxes consume 1.34% of median household income ($39,454), a ratio that falls within generally sustainable thresholds but warrants attention given the parish's income profile. Over a 30-year ownership horizon, cumulative property tax payments on a median-valued home ($118,800) total approximately $15,900—an amount equivalent to 40% of a single year's median household income. This long-term cost represents a material financial commitment for households operating with limited fiscal margin. Prospective buyers should recognize that low nominal tax bills correlate with modest assessed values and limited service provision. Current owners should verify assessment accuracy annually, as Louisiana's reassessment cycles can produce valuation discrepancies. A notable insight: the parish's tax-to-income ratio (1.34%) exceeds that of many higher-rate jurisdictions with stronger income bases, suggesting that low statutory rates do not automatically translate to low relative burden when local earning capacity is constrained.
