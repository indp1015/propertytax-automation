📝 Property Tax Notes

The effective property‑tax rate in Suwannee County stands at 0.71 %, which is 0.21 percentage points below the national average of 0.92 % and 0.71 percentage points above the reported Florida state “average” of 0.00 % (the state figure reflects the absence of a statewide property‑tax levy, with rates set locally). Relative to other U.S. jurisdictions, Suwannee County therefore qualifies as a low‑tax area; the median annual levy of $1,213 is roughly 26 % less than the national median of $1,630. When expressed as a share of median household income ($56,658), the property‑tax burden equals 2.14 % of earnings. This proportion is modest compared with many states where property taxes consume 3 %–5 % of income, suggesting that the current level is financially sustainable for the median household. The 5‑year, 10‑year and 30‑year cumulative burdens—$6,065, $12,130 and $36,390 respectively—translate to an average annual outlay that remains well under one‑quarter of a typical household’s yearly income. For a buyer targeting a median‑priced home ($171,600), the projected 30‑year tax cost of $36,390 represents roughly 21 % of the purchase price, or an effective annual cost of $1,213. Prospective owners should therefore factor this steady, predictable expense into cash‑flow calculations but need not anticipate a disproportionate tax drag on long‑term affordability. Current owners can explore common Florida exemptions such as the homestead exemption, which can reduce assessed value by up to $50,000, and the “Save Our Homes” cap that limits annual assessment increases to 3 % or the inflation rate, whichever is lower. A less obvious insight is that the low effective rate is partially offset by the county’s relatively low median home value; the combination yields a tax burden that is both low in absolute dollars and modest as an income share, reinforcing Suwannee County’s overall affordability profile for both new entrants and long‑term residents.
