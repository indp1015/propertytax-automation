📝 Property Tax Notes

Washington County’s effective property‑tax rate of 0.39 percent places it well below the national average of 0.92 percent and marginally above the state‑wide figure, which is reported as 0.00 percent (reflecting Colorado’s generally low rates). Relative to the nation, the county’s rate is 0.53 percentage points lower, confirming that Washington County is a low‑tax jurisdiction. The median annual levy of $874 on a median home valued at $226,200 translates to an effective burden of 1.30 percent of the median household income of $67,167, indicating that property tax consumes a modest share of earnings. When expressed as a share of income, the 1.30 percent contribution is comfortably sustainable for most households, especially when compared with the national median tax‑to‑income ratio, which typically exceeds 2 percent. Over a 30‑year holding period, the cumulative tax outlay on a median‑priced residence amounts to $26,220 (30 × $874), or roughly 12 percent of the home’s original value. This long‑term cost remains modest relative to total housing expenses, suggesting that property‑tax considerations are unlikely to dominate affordability calculations for buyers. Prospective homeowners should factor the low tax rate into their overall budgeting but also verify any supplemental levies tied to school districts, fire protection or special assessments, which can vary within the county. Current owners may benefit from Colorado’s property‑tax exemptions for seniors, veterans, and disabled persons, as well as the state’s “circuit‑breaker” credit that caps tax liability at a percentage of household income. While eligibility criteria differ, these programs can further reduce the effective rate for qualifying filers. A less obvious insight emerges from the disparity between the county’s low effective rate and the relatively high median home value for the region. The modest tax burden is achieved not by low property values but by comparatively low millage, meaning homeowners enjoy both appreciable equity and limited annual tax outlays—a combination that can enhance long‑term wealth accumulation without imposing a heavy fiscal strain.
